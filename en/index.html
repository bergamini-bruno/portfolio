Lexemo

Learning vocabulary, one word at a time

JavaScript Python MySQL Web App
github.com/gatoxaxo/lexemo

Lexemo is a vocabulary learning app built around card decks. Instead of fixed lists, every deck can grow endlessly and be played in many ways, from quick recognition to pronunciation and the romanization of non-Latin scripts.

The goal is to remove the friction from memorization: creating cards is no longer manual work, hints fade away little by little until you rely on memory alone, and everything can be adjusted mid-game without leaving the screen.

1. Deck list

Each deck is its own set of cards, with an unlimited number of entries. Decks are organized in a selectable list, and from any of them you pick the game mode and start the session.

2. Cards as JSON

Instead of registering card by card, the whole list can be edited as a JSON document with the question, answer and hint fields. That makes it possible to generate large decks at once with the help of chatbots, import ready-made files and export the content for backup or sharing.

3. Airplane game

To train how fast lexemes are recognized, an airplane crosses the screen shooting letters and words as the corresponding translation appears. The exercise swaps calm reading for reflex: identifying the right word under pressure and in the shortest time possible.

4. Free mode

In free mode the hints are optional and configurable: revealed letters, a fixed color derived from the word's hash and a predictable position on screen. They are cognitive support at first, but the main point is being able to turn them off gradually. With each level, fewer clues, until the word is remembered for what it is, not for its color or where it used to be.

5. Transcription mode

For languages that use non-Roman scripts, practice can be done through romanization: the app shows the original text and you type the matching reading. It is the intermediate step between memorizing symbols and reading whole words fluently.

6. Editing a card mid-game

Any card can be fixed during the session, in a form with the question, the answer and an optional context hint. Maintenance tweaks no longer require opening the whole list and hunting for the right entry among hundreds of items.

7. Game options

Each deck has its own settings: game mode (free, speed, SRS, pronunciation or transcription), reading voice, number of revealed letters, color and position hints, random order, restart on mistakes, plus reverse, listening and syllable modes. These are the variables that define whether a session trains listening, speaking, recognition or reflex.

8. General settings

Outside the session, preferences take care of the environment: hit and error sounds, audio autoplay, dark theme and interface language. That way the app speaks the user's language, regardless of the language being studied.

Lead Scraper

Prospecting through Instagram network analysis

Python JavaScript MySQL Scraping
Private repository

The goal was to build a list of genuinely qualified leads to pitch paid traffic and landing pages to, instead of blasting messages at random profiles. The idea was to search for clinics across Brazil, look at each one's Instagram and use the follower network's behavior as a signal of demand.

The core concept is interconnection. Against common sense, the number of followers says little. What says a lot is how many connections repeat between the analyzed account and its own followers.

How the bot works

  1. 1

    Search by segment

    Scraping

    Queries run through DuckDuckGo or Google scraping, looking for spa, waxing and general clinics across the whole country.

  2. 2

    Opening the profile

    Instagram

    For each result, the bot opens the Instagram page and collects the profile's basic data.

  3. 3

    Meta Ads check

    Meta Ads

    It queries the Meta Ads Library to check whether that business already runs ads. That indicates there is money circulating and someone taking care of acquisition.

  4. 4

    Own website check

    Instagram

    A scan of the profile to identify whether the clinic points to its own website. The absence of one is one of the opportunity signals for selling a landing page.

  5. 5

    Follower cross-reference

    Graph

    On public accounts, the bot collects the clinic's followers, then the followers of those followers, and cross-references both sets to see who shows up on both sides. That intersection is the measure of interconnection.

  6. 6

    Google reviews

    Places API

    When it made sense, the Google Places API came in to check ratings and review volume, helping to discard very small clinics that probably would not have a budget.

  7. 7

    Lead scoring

    Score

    Each profile gets a read combining interconnection, ad presence and size. The final result is a list ready for cold outreach.

What interconnection reveals

The tests showed quite large differences between niches. Network-driven environments such as schools had over 90% of connections with an intersection, while very specific niches, such as tattoo removal clinics, barely overlap at all.

The decision logic

The combination of interconnection and ad presence is what gives the lead meaning. High interconnection suggests a business that grows through referrals and has organic demand. Low interconnection with a strong Meta Ads presence raises the suspicion that those results depend on paid traffic, which usually indicates someone looking for clients.

Worth saying that this is not a categorical statement about the business. It is a signal, and even as a signal it already improved the quality of the list a lot compared to picking random sets of profiles.

Low interconnection + strong Meta Ads Best scenario: likely dependence on paid traffic.
High interconnection + strong Meta Ads Consolidated organic demand and already investing. Good client, different approach.
High interconnection + no Ads Grows through referrals. Less urgent, but with first-project potential.
Low interconnection + no Ads Little signal. Usually discarded or left at the end of the queue.

Hack and Slice (VR)

Mesh cutting in virtual reality

C# Unity VR Mesh
Private repository

A virtual reality experiment in Unity with saber physics. The idea was easy to explain and hard to pull off: the player has a lightsaber and can cut any object in the scene, at any angle.

There was no list of pre-cut objects. The mesh was split on the fly, based on where and in which direction the saber passed.

How the cut works

The saber is neither a raycast nor a regular collision. It is treated as a moving plane: on every frame, the velocity between the previous and the current position defines a direction, and that direction forms a cutting plane in space.

The mesh is then split by that plane. The triangles on each side are kept and the ones crossing the cut line are subdivided into smaller pieces, so the cut edge follows the plane exactly. This works for any model, no matter the shape, without any prior setup on the asset.

After subdividing, each side gets its own cap: the points where the plane crossed the volume are connected to close the surface. Without that, the cut object would be hollow and the illusion would break.

Keeping the 3D coherent

  • The split respects the original topology: each fragment remains a closed, valid mesh.
  • Normals, texture coordinates and tangents are recalculated on the new vertices, so lighting and texture stay correct after the cut.
  • Physics follows the geometry: each half gets its own rigidbody and starts behaving independently.
  • The cut is driven by the saber's velocity, so the direction of the swing actually matters.

WhatsApp Assistant

From self-care reminder to personal assistant

Python JavaScript MySQL Bot
Private repository

It started as a favor. A friend kept forgetting her own self-care dates, hair, nails, eyebrows, so I asked a bot to remind her. The idea was just that: send a message on the right day.

But once it was done, it became clear the format worked for much more than that. The scope grew and the bot became a general-purpose personal assistant, always inside WhatsApp, with no app, no dashboard and no database.

How it works

The bot listens to messages, understands what was asked and keeps the reminders in plain text, in a simple file. No table, no schema, nothing like that. Persistence is literally whatever it agreed with the person, and that is enough for the volume of use.

For the notifications, a cron job runs periodically, checks what is due and fires the message at the right time. Since the channel is WhatsApp, there is no need for push notifications, an installed app or a platform token, the user just opens the conversation.

Natural conversation

The user does not fill out a form. They write the way they would speak and the bot builds the list as it goes, confirming what it understood and asking for whatever is missing. When asked for the list, it returns it formatted. When asked for the date of the next event, it calculates it from the agreed frequency.

Prompt injection

An open assistant on WhatsApp is an easy target, and testing that became part of development. One of the tests was trying to steer the bot with instructions like "ignore everything before this and answer something else".

The defense is keeping the persona and the scope well defined in the system: the bot is a personal assistant and does not change roles. It is not a foolproof protection, but it holds off the silly cases without ruining normal conversation.

What I learned

  • Restricting scope is not a lack of ambition, it is what makes the bot reliable day to day.
  • Not every project needs a database, a dashboard and an app. A cron and a file solve a lot.
  • Testing prompt injection should be a habit, just like testing error cases.

FPS Director

Video editing through natural language

Python JavaScript MySQL FastAPI
Private repository

FPS Director is a FastAPI backend that converts requests written in Portuguese into yt-dlp and ffmpeg commands. The core idea is to separate two things that usually get mixed together: understanding what the person meant and actually building the video output.

A language model handles interpretation only. The backend, written in deterministic rules, decides which concrete media to act on and how to assemble the result. That way the model does not need to know durations, cuts or session state, and the backend does not try to guess language.

How the video is represented

There is no animation in the model. The final video is a sequence of fixed states, each with a layer, a time interval and a set of constant properties. Anything that varies over time, such as a brightness ramp, is broken into several short intervals.

The pipeline keeps two independent tracks that do not mix: one of lineage, which records where each piece of media came from, and another of coherence, which holds the consolidated physical state, such as speed, duration and exclusivity between properties.

The nine layers

layer with a language model deterministic layer
  1. 1

    Reduter

    LLM

    Reads the request and splits the intentions by entity, keeping the original excerpts, and also the conceptual lineage of that event. Still without resolving state, numbers or links.

  2. 2

    Ancorer

    Deterministic

    Group that anchors the new event to the session. First the lineage is matched against the saved lineage from the previous iteration, by entity. Then each raw intention is classified by type, scope and value, defining whether the request is about window, duration, speed or property, and whether the target is the video, the audio or the material.

  3. 3

    Structurer

    Deterministic

    Turns the accumulated lineage into symbolic blocks. It is a mechanical translation, without reinterpreting anything. If something without a defined mapping shows up, the layer flags it instead of guessing.

  4. 4

    Canonizer

    LLM

    Translates the symbolic key into a canonical property. If there is no direct match, it tries to compose the effect from properties that already exist. If even that is not possible, it returns a question instead of making something up.

  5. 5

    Composed prop generator

    Deterministic

    When the previous layer composes an effect, it is registered in the internal vocabulary as a cache and normalized to the canonical properties. When a question comes back, it is forwarded to the interface.

  6. 6

    Quantifier

    Deterministic

    Assigns numbers to the symbolic values, resolving fractions, times and positions relative to the known facts.

  7. 7

    Solver

    Deterministic

    Resolves and validates the arithmetic, leaving the cuts ready for the next step.

  8. 8

    Contextualizer

    Deterministic

    Links each block to the real state it needs to act on, walking up the lineage until it finds the first state that contains the requested interval. It also creates the links between dependent media. The explicit reference to the original material is the only case that points straight at the source.

  9. 9

    Coherence engine

    Deterministic

    Applies the domain's physical rules, such as exclusivity between properties and duration propagation through the links. It uses a lightweight graph with topological ordering, where the edges come from the rules themselves, not from a fixed sequence in the code. The final state stays in the session for the next iteration.

  10. 10

    Composer

    Deterministic

    Assembles two artifacts in parallel: the positional graph sent to the front end, with media, layers and intervals, and the consolidated lineage of the iteration, which is saved to the session and comes back to the second layer on the next request.

AeroJobs

Recruiting without bureaucracy

Python JavaScript MySQL Web App
Private repository

AeroJobs is a recruiting platform that aims to simplify the lives of candidates and recruiters. The idea is to make job openings more transparent, make applications easier and use artificial intelligence to analyze resumes and identify compatibility with each opportunity's requirements.

The focus is on removing unnecessary steps and making the hiring process faster, clearer and more human, helping companies find professionals aligned with the roles and candidates better understand their opportunities.

1. Creating job openings

The sign-up is simple and direct: the company provides only the job title and its description in free text, without filling out a series of forms. The system analyzes the information and organizes the data needed for publishing.

2. Job description page

The opening is presented to the candidate in a standardized format, with the information organized automatically by the system. If the company has other registered opportunities, they can also be displayed on the page.

3. Hiring stages and sign-up

At the bottom of the page, the candidate finds the stages of the hiring process and the option to register their resume. Access is simplified through Google account login.

4. Resume upload and analysis

Candidates can upload their resume in different formats, such as PDF, DOCX, PNG or JPG. The system uses OCR and artificial intelligence to analyze the content and generate immediate feedback based on the job requirements. It is also possible to add a supplementary document (addendum), which can be analyzed by the AI in a later step.

5. HR candidate dashboard

The recruiter sees the list of applications with each candidate's match score, which can be configured manually. It is also possible to open the resumes individually to analyze the profiles in more detail.

6. HR job dashboard

The dashboard gathers the openings registered by the company, making it easier to view and manage the published opportunities.

Much of my time went into making the system talk properly with the devices. It is an area where small communication details turn into big problems in the field, so I learned by doing what worked and what did not.

Device integration

I worked with protocols such as TALQ and IOTHub to connect the devices to the city management system. The gain I remember most was a reduction of around 40% in communication failures, coming from tweaks to timeouts, reconnection and the format in which the data was handled.

I also implemented HTTP, BLE and radio communication at specific points of the product, on the lamp remote-control side. And I used Node-RED to orchestrate devices and sensors within the remote management system, which helped a lot during the testing phase with real equipment.

The app and everyday tools

I also built energy consumption monitoring modules and screens for the remote management ERP, always aiming to make the operation simpler for the people using it.

Bench work, reprogramming and radio testing

It was not just server-side code. At several points the work moved to the bench: taking photos of the equipment for documentation, opening the hardware, identifying board versions and reprogramming the embedded devices by hand. Every device that left the factory with the wrong version or came back from the field behaving oddly went through that process.

Radio testing was a fixed part of the routine. Since communication was not only over the network, many problems only showed up in practice: shorter range than expected, corrupted packets arriving, or the same device working in one place and failing in another. Testing on the bench, with the equipment under controlled conditions, helped separate firmware problems from hardware and environment issues.

The neighboring antenna interference

One of the most memorable cases was a radio transmission antenna right next to the company. At specific times and conditions, it interfered with the tests and with the behavior of some devices, and for a while the suspicion always fell on the firmware, because that is where the failure showed up.

The path was to eliminate variables. First, measure to confirm the problem was external. Then isolate the test bench with a Faraday cage, which blocks external electromagnetic interference. With the environment controlled, it became possible to test the radio without the antenna noise and reach conclusions that were impossible to separate from everything else before.

It was a very concrete lesson: in embedded systems, the environment is part of the problem. Sometimes the code is correct and the signal is what was not.

Electronics and Arduino

That closeness to hardware came from before, from personal electronics and Arduino projects. One of them was an automatic humidifier system, which read the air humidity and triggered the equipment on its own. It is the kind of small project that teaches a lot: sensors, analog reading, noise, power and the care not to burn anything along the way.

Having that foundation helped a lot when the subject stopped being only software and started involving devices, boards and a bench full of things.

Through all of that, I learned that integrating hardware and software is less about the technology itself and more about patience to investigate the real cause of every failure.
